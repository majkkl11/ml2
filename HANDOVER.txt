2. BEFORE YOU SPLIT: THREE DECISIONS
==============================================================================

DECISION 1: split by time, never randomly.
  A random split trains on matches played after the ones it is tested on.
  Suggested: train on seasons up to 2022/23, validate on 2023/24, test on
  2024/25. The season column is a string: "1920" = 2019/20, ..., "2425".
  Tune and choose everything on the validation season. Touch the test season
  once, at the end.

DECISION 2: what to do with 2019/20.
  It is a warm-up season for the features: 8.7% of the 10-match form values
  are missing (under 0.5% in later seasons), only 1% of matches have 40 past
  matches behind them, and every Elo rating starts at 1500. Either keep it in
  training with weaker features, or use it only as history and train from
  2020/21. Try both on the validation season.

DECISION 3: accuracy per country is noisy.
  The price of the data is set per market (country), so you need the accuracy
  of both models per country. Matches per country in 2024/25:
    England 2036, Spain 842, Scotland 768, Italy 760, France 612, Germany 611,
    Turkey 341, Belgium 311, Netherlands 306, Portugal 306, Greece 233.
  With about 300 matches, chance alone moves an accuracy by roughly 3
  percentage points either way. The gap between the two models may be smaller
  than that. Testing on two seasons (2023/24 and 2024/25) halves the problem
  but costs training data. Decide this before you look at any test result,
  and say in the notebook how reliable the per-country numbers are.

CONSTRAINT: the Elo settings were tuned on 2020/21 to 2022/23.
  K, home advantage and the division-change rule were chosen on those three
  seasons. They must stay in the training set. If you move the split earlier,
  the Elo settings have seen validation or test data. 2022/23 has to be in the
  training set.

COVID: matches behind closed doors (section 3.7).
  From May 2020 to June 2021 most matches were played without spectators.
  Home wins fell from about 44% to 41% and over 2.5 goals from about 50% to
  48%. The odds had already priced this in. The whole period lies in the
  training seasons; validation and test are normal seasons with crowds.
  - Do not drop both 2019/20 and this period from training: only about
    14,600 training matches would remain.
  - The baseline feature closed_doors (1 for these matches) lets the model
    treat the period separately. It is 0 in validation and test, and it is
    known before kick-off, so it is no leak. Keep it only if it helps on the
    validation season. Training without the period is the alternative.
  - If you drop these matches, drop them from baseline_data / extended_data,
    never from data before section 4: they are the history for later form
    features and Elo ratings.
  - The Elo home advantage of 40 is a compromise between 30 (behind closed
    doors) and 50 (with crowds); see the test after the Elo cell in 4.2.

3. THE MODEL
==============================================================================

The plan is XGBoost (binary classification of over_2_5).

Missing values: leave them. Do not impute and do not fill with 0.
  XGBoost learns at every split which way to send missing values. Missing
  shares are small: about 1.7% for most form features, 3.4% for the home-only
  and away-only form, 2% for the matchup features, under 1% for the odds
  features. Most gaps are structural (a team has fewer than 3 past matches),
  and filling them with a mean or 0 would claim the team is average or
  scoreless. If you also fit a model that cannot handle missing values (for
  example logistic regression), impute the finished features with values
  fitted on the training set only.

Feature names: XGBoost rejects names containing [, ] or <. All 88 feature
  names are safe. The raw odds columns (for example Avg<2.5) are not, but
  they are not in the two datasets.

Not included as features: the division or country itself. div_goals_365d
  carries the scoring level of the division. You can add Div as a categorical
  feature if you want to test it; it would be a baseline feature.

Metrics: report accuracy, because the assignment's profit model uses it (A0
  and A1). Also look at log loss when comparing variants, because it is less
  jumpy than accuracy.

Reference point: the market alone is right 57.4% of the time. That is the
  accuracy of predicting "over" whenever prob_over_avg is above 0.5, and it
  is between 56% and 58% in every season. The baseline model has no odds, so
  expect it clearly below that. The extended model should reach about that
  level; if it beats it, the match statistics add something beyond the odds.
  Expect accuracies in the mid to high 50s and a small gap between the two
  models. An accuracy far above 60% most likely means leakage.

Feature importance: planned after training, to see which of the 88 features
  matter and which can go. Candidates to turn out useless: elo_diff, the fouls
  and yellow card features, prob_over_gap.

5. THE FEATURES (section 4)
==============================================================================

Every form feature is an average over a team's PREVIOUS matches. The current
match is never included. Windows: 10 matches (recent form), 40 matches
(long-term strength), 5 matches (home-only or away-only form). A feature
stays missing until the team has 3 earlier matches. Form carries over from
one season to the next. Team-level features exist twice, with the prefix
home_ and away_.

BASELINE (41), built only from Div, Date, HomeTeam, AwayTeam, FTHG, FTAG, FTR
(country, league and season follow from Div and Date):
  goals_for, goals_against, points          windows 10 and 40, per team
  over_share, clean_sheet_share,
    failed_to_score_share                   window 10, per team
  venue_goals_for, venue_goals_against,
    venue_points                            window 5, per team
  days_since_last_match                     per team
  matches_in_data                           per team; past matches in the data,
                                            capped at 40
  season_stage                              matches played / scheduled length
  div_goals_365d                            goals per match in the division,
                                            previous 365 days
  matchup_home_goals, matchup_away_goals,
    matchup_total_goals                     windows 10 and 40; attack of one
                                            team averaged with defence of the other
  home_elo, away_elo, elo_diff, elo_abs_diff
  closed_doors                              1 for matches behind closed doors
                                            (May 2020 - June 2021), else 0

EXTENDED (47):
  shots, shots on target, corners,
    for and against                         windows 10 and 40, per team
  fouls committed and suffered,
    yellow cards own and opponent's         window 40 only, per team
  matchup shots on target (home, away,
    total)                                  windows 10 and 40
  prob_over_avg, prob_over_pinnacle,
    prob_over_gap                           over 2.5 probability from the odds,
                                            bookmaker margin removed
  prob_home, prob_draw, prob_away,
    prob_favourite                          from the market average odds
  handicap, handicap_abs                    Asian handicap of the home team

Settings you may be asked about:
  - Elo: start 1500, K = 20, home advantage 40 points; a promoted or new team
    starts 50 points below the average of its new division, a relegated team
    50 above. Ratings are only comparable within a division.
  - season_stage: scheduled length is two matches against every other team,
    with a small table of exceptions for Scotland, Greece and Belgium (from
    2023/24). The Greek and Belgian play-off lengths were read off the match
    counts in the data and are worth a second look.
  - Not built: red cards, shot quality, head-to-head history, calendar
    features (month, weekday, kick-off hour).

Known limitation: a promoted or relegated team's form averages were earned in
another division. Elo corrects for this, the averages do not.
